A working list for small-business owners who need systems, not another dashboard. I bias toward tools that a 2–20 person team can actually adopt: affordable, common enough that you can hire help, and boring in a good way.

This is not comprehensive. If I skipped something you love—or if a tool here has gone sideways—[email me](mailto:dave@davidcmitchell.com).

_Last updated August 2026._

## Bookkeeping

- [QuickBooks Online](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/) — The default in the US. Your CPA, bookkeeper, and payroll person already know it. Start here unless you have a strong reason not to.
- [Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/) — Cleaner day-to-day than QuickBooks for a lot of owners. Strong if your accountant is already on it.
- [Wave](https://www.waveapps.com/) — Free books and invoicing. Fine when you’re tiny. You’ll outgrow it.
- [FreshBooks](https://www.freshbooks.com/) — Invoicing-first. Better for service/freelance billing than for a full set of books.

Skip the clever newcomers until your chart of accounts is boring and correct.

## Payroll

- [Gusto](https://gusto.com/) — Straightforward payroll and onboarding for small US teams.
- [QuickBooks Payroll](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/payroll/) — Convenient if you’re already in QuickBooks. Don’t pick it just to “keep it in one place” if Gusto is clearly easier for you.

## Payments

- [Stripe](https://stripe.com/) — Cards and online invoices. The default if you bill from a website or send payment links.
- [Square](https://squareup.com/) — In-person and simple invoicing. Common for service businesses that take a card on site.
- [PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/) — Customers still ask for it. Keep it as an option, not the whole stack.

## Scheduling, jobs, and field work

If you have techs, trucks, or recurring jobs, a field-service tool often _is_ your CRM. Don’t buy a separate CRM until this layer is working.

- [Jobber](https://getjobber.com/) — Quotes, scheduling, invoicing, and payments for small home-service teams. The usual starting point.
- [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/) — Similar job: dispatch, booking, invoices, customer comms. Compare it to Jobber; don’t run both.
- [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/) — Serious ops software. Usually too much (and too expensive) until you have a real dispatch function and the volume to match.

## ERP / all-in-one

- [ERPNext](https://erpnext.com/) — Open-source ERP (accounting, inventory, CRM, HR, manufacturing, projects). Self-host or run on Frappe Cloud. Worth a look if you’re trying to replace a pile of overlapping tools and you’re willing to implement it like a system, not install it like an app. It is not a substitute for Jobber/Housecall if the actual job is dispatching techs.

## CRM (when you actually need a separate one)

Most owners in this size range don’t. If sales still lives in a spreadsheet and nobody owns follow-up, then:

- [HubSpot CRM](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) — Free tier is enough to stop losing leads. Don’t turn on the whole HubSpot suite on day one.
- [Notion](https://www.notion.so/) — Fine as a lightweight pipeline if the team already lives there. It’s not a CRM; don’t pretend it is.

## Communication

- [Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/) — Email on your domain, Calendar, Drive, Docs. This is the floor.
- [Slack](https://slack.com/) — Team chat once email threads are getting in the way. Keep channels few and named after work, not vibes.
- [Zoom](https://zoom.us/) — Still the default for calls with people outside the company.

ImprovMX is still a cheap way to forward `you@yourdomain.com` to wherever you actually read mail, if you’re not ready for full Workspace.

## Projects and tasks

- [Asana](https://asana.com/) — Shared work, owners, and due dates. Good once “it’s in my head” stops working.
- [Trello](https://trello.com/) — Simple boards. Fine for a single pipeline; messy as the company brain.
- [Google Calendar](https://calendar.google.com/) — If the business doesn’t have a shared calendar, start there before you buy a PM tool.

## Time tracking

- [Harvest](https://www.getharvest.com/) — Time plus light invoicing. Useful if you bill hours or need to know where the week went.
- [Toggl Track](https://toggl.com/track/) — Fast to start. Better for personal/team time than for payroll compliance.
- [Hubstaff](https://hubstaff.com/) — Time plus optional location/activity. Use it if you have a real need; it changes the feel of the team.

## Email and lists

- [Kit](https://kit.com/) — (formerly ConvertKit) Email for creators and operators who write. This is what I use.
- [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/) — Fine if you already live there. Don’t migrate just to migrate.

## Contracts and signatures

- [Dropbox Sign](https://www.dropbox.com/sign) — (HelloSign) Send a contract, get it signed, file it. That’s the job.
- [PandaDoc](https://www.pandadoc.com/) — Heavier: proposals plus signature. Worth it when quotes are a real sales process.

Have a lawyer draft the actual MSA/terms. Software does not replace that.

## What I usually skip

- Closed all-in-one “run your whole company” suites (Bitrix24 and friends) — you spend a year configuring instead of documenting the work. ERPNext is the exception I keep on the list because you can own the system; it still isn’t a weekend project.
- Niche CRMs nobody else knows — hiring and handoff get harder.
- Recruiting marketplaces aimed at tech (Hired, etc.) — wrong problem for most small operators.
- A second tool that duplicates Jobber/Housecall/QuickBooks. One system of record per job (money, customers, schedule).
