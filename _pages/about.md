## I thought I knew how to run an operation.

I spent 23 years in software—writing code, leading teams, managing projects, building products. I earned an MBA from BYU, became a Certified Scrum Master, led groups as large as a few hundred people, and partnered in [nStar Global Services](http://www.nstarglobal.com). I specialized in development process and team leadership.

Then I bought a franchise. And the theory met the truck.

---

## The Conserva years

In 2020 I bought a [Conserva Irrigation](https://conservairrigation.com/) franchise and ran it myself. No team. No safety net. Just me and a set of systems I had to build from scratch.

Over six years I grew it to 1,500+ homes and a 95%+ customer retention rate. I was proud of it. I was also wearing every hat—sales, operations, scheduling, customer service, accounting. I was the bottleneck.

I knew how to teach delegation. Living it inside a small business with real money on the line is different.

So I started building systems. Documenting processes. Finding tools that could take the repetitive work. Figuring out which tasks actually needed me and which ones just needed clarity.

The business got better. I got time back. I could take a week off without my phone lighting up constantly.

That’s the work I now do with other owners.

## What I believe

**Systems beat heroics.** The owner who works 80-hour weeks isn’t a hero—they’re a single point of failure.

**Your business should work without you.** Not someday. Now. If you left for a month and the wheels came off, that’s a systems problem.

**Balance is possible.** I’m an Aikido black belt (20+ years), a science fiction and fantasy writer, a mountain biker, a scuba diver, and a tabletop RPG player. I practice what I preach because I built the systems to make it possible.

**Nerd culture is a leadership toolkit.** Aikido’s habit of redirecting force. Chess’s habit of thinking several moves ahead. The way a good tabletop campaign actually runs a team. These aren’t decorations. They’re how I explain the work.
